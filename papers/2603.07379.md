## Problem

Agentic RAG systems are described using overlapping labels—adaptive, corrective, multi-agent, GraphRAG, tool-using, planning—without a stable framework for comparing what is actually controlled or how trajectories should be evaluated.

## Core idea

View Agentic RAG as a finite-horizon partially observable decision process: the system observes partial state, chooses a retrieval/tool action, receives an observation, updates state, and eventually stops/generates. Use that lens to systematize architectures, evaluation, risks, and research directions.

## Agent loop

`Observe state → choose retrieval/tool action → observe evidence → update state → repeat → generate/stop`

## Retrieval design

This is a systematization, not a new retriever. Its value comes from making **control policy and trajectory** central units for comparing retrieval orchestration and tool invocation.

## Compared to what

Prior RAG taxonomies organized mainly by retriever components, pipeline stages, or application categories, plus static RAG evaluation that focuses on final answer/retrieval metrics without explicitly modeling the action trajectory.

## Evidence

The abstract describes literature systematization and risk analysis rather than a benchmarked new method. The current radar has not yet audited coverage against prior Agentic RAG surveys or checked whether the sequential-decision framing leads to materially sharper taxonomy/evaluation categories.

## Why it matters

The framing is useful because it matches the design stack emerging from method papers: **operation set, state, policy, and stopping**. If those variables are made explicit, evaluation can ask which part of the trajectory actually changed rather than attribute every system-level gain to a generic “agentic” label.

Its lasting value therefore depends on whether the systematization improves comparison and failure analysis, not on introducing new terminology.

## Limitations / questions

- How novel is the taxonomy relative to earlier Agentic RAG surveys?
- Does the decision-process formalization lead to concrete trajectory-level metrics or benchmark design?
- How comprehensively does the SoK cover database, web, graph, code, and multimodal retrieval environments?
- Does it separate retrieval primitive, state, policy, and budget clearly enough to diagnose causal improvements?

**Curator take:** **3/5**. Useful organizing anchor, but the radar should not give survey/systematization papers high importance until coverage, novelty, and practical evaluative consequences are verified.
