# SPARKLE: A Structured and Plug-and-play Agentic Retrieval Policy for Adaptive RAG Models

**Paper:** [ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1793/) · **Published online:** 2026-06-24 · **Category:** Learning & Optimization · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High · **Code:** [jyfang6/sparkle](https://github.com/jyfang6/sparkle)

**Tags:** `adaptive RAG` `reinforcement learning` `retriever routing` `query formulation` `knowledge graph` `plug-and-play`

> **TL;DR.** SPARKLE learns adaptive retrieval control in a separate proxy policy—retrieval decision, query formulation, and knowledge integration—while treating the retriever and answer LLM as a fixed/swappable environment.

## Problem

Two common adaptive-RAG regimes create opposite coupling problems: prompt-only controllers are weakly supervised, while finetuning the main answer LLM is expensive and ties policy learning to one generator/retriever stack.

## Core idea

Introduce a separate proxy model with three structured roles: **Retrieval Decision**, **Query Formulation**, and **Knowledge Integration**. Train the proxy with SFT and PPO while the retriever and answer LLM remain part of the environment; binary tree-structured rollouts explore alternative retrieval trajectories during RL.

## Agent loop

`answer-model reasoning state → proxy decides retrieve? → formulate query → retrieve → integrate knowledge → update reasoning → repeat or answer`

## Retrieval design

The controller uses KG-based structured reasoning and is deliberately decoupled from the underlying retriever and answer model. The paper evaluates transfer by changing retrievers and LLMs without retraining the policy.

## Compared to what

The important baselines are not static RAG. SPARKLE compares against adaptive/search policies including Adaptive-RAG, Search-o1, Search-R1, and R1-Searcher, which makes it a stronger reference point for learned retrieval policy than static-vs-adaptive papers.

## Evidence

The ACL paper reports average improvements of **9.17% in-domain** and **2.85% out-of-domain** over state-of-the-art adaptive RAG baselines across seven QA benchmarks. The matched-generator result is more informative than the headline: when the answer model is held to Qwen2.5-7B, SPARKLE is competitive but not uniformly best against every RL search baseline.

That distinction matters. The evidence supports **plug-and-play learned control and transfer** more cleanly than a claim that one proxy-policy architecture universally dominates adaptive search.

## Why it matters

SPARKLE closes an evaluation hole in the radar's learning map. It shows that a learned retrieval controller should be judged against other adaptive policies—not only against static k or prompt-only retrieval—and gives a clean architectural separation between **policy** and **answer-model capacity**.

## Limitations / questions

- KG construction and the proxy architecture add machinery beyond RL, so end-to-end gains are not an RL-only ablation.
- Stronger answer models still improve the same policy, preserving a base-model confound.
- Plug-and-play transfer is promising, but broader workloads beyond QA would be needed to establish a general agentic retrieval policy.

**Curator take:** **4/5**. Important as a strong adaptive-policy baseline and as evidence that policy learning can be decoupled from the answer LLM; less convincing as a universal winner over all search-RL alternatives.
