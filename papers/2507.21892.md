## Problem

Conventional GraphRAG often retrieves or expands graph context once and then relies on prompting over the result. The model therefore does not learn how intermediate graph access should change as reasoning progresses.

## Core idea

Treat the graph as an interactive retrieval environment, expose retrieval as a sequence of actions and observations, and optimize the whole retrieval/reasoning trajectory with outcome-based reinforcement learning.

## Agent loop

`Reason → choose graph retrieval action → observe graph evidence → update state → repeat → answer`

Training attaches outcome reward to the trajectory rather than evaluating each retrieval only as an isolated relevance event.

## Retrieval design

Graph retrieval is part of the policy. Rather than precomputing one context expansion, the agent repeatedly interacts with graph structure as its information need evolves.

## Compared to what

Traditional GraphRAG, fixed one-shot graph retrieval, and systems where RL improves reasoning but graph retrieval remains a static component.

## Evidence

The paper reports improvements in reasoning accuracy, retrieval efficiency, and generation quality on standard RAG datasets. The current radar note has not yet re-checked the complete experimental tables, reward design, or ablations separating graph representation quality from learned retrieval policy.

## Why it matters

Graph-R1 is a useful design anchor because it makes the **retrieval trajectory** itself a policy-learning object. That connects GraphRAG with the broader Agentic RAG question: once the retrieval environment exposes meaningful actions, should control be prompted, planned, or learned?

It also complements Agentic-R: Graph-R1 primarily learns the agent's graph-interaction trajectory, while Agentic-R asks how the retriever itself should be trained for trajectory utility.

## Limitations / questions

- Outcome-level reward may provide weak credit assignment for intermediate retrieval actions.
- How much gain comes from the graph representation versus the learned policy?
- Are retrieval/token budgets matched against fixed GraphRAG baselines?
- Does the learned policy transfer across graph schemas or domains?

**Curator take:** **4/5**. Important as a clean bridge between GraphRAG and policy learning; the full-paper audit should focus on credit assignment, budget fairness, and representation/policy attribution.
