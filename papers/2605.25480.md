# Retrieval as Reasoning: Self-Evolving Agent-Native Retrieval via LLM-Wiki

**arXiv:** [2605.25480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480) · **Published:** 2026-05-25 · **Category:** Retrieval & Tool Use · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High

**Tags:** `documents` `tool use` `iterative search` `adaptive stopping` `multi-hop QA`

> **TL;DR.** LLM-Wiki is an important pre-August anchor for agent-native retrieval: compile documents into linked, inspectable Wiki pages, expose `search` and `read` as composable tools, let links discovered during reading drive later hops, and maintain the structure through a persistent Error Book. Crucially, its ablation suggests the **progressive traversal policy adds value beyond the compiled structure itself**.

## Problem

In multi-hop QA, semantically distant intermediate entities are often the evidence bottleneck. A dense retriever can retrieve the two entities named in the question while missing a biography or relation page needed for the next hop. Repeated similarity search does not automatically make that relation discoverable.

## Core idea

LLM-Wiki moves structure to **compile time**. Raw source passages are incrementally converted into structured Markdown Wiki pages with aliases, tags, key facts, source references, and bidirectional links. At query time, the agent operates over this maintained surface rather than a flat chunk list.

The paper calls the design principles:

`compilability → composability → evolvability`

## Agent loop

`search or browse → read page/index → follow links or reformulate → check evidence sufficiency → repeat or answer`

The concrete tool interface is deliberately small: `wiki_search(query)` and `wiki_read(paths)`. A read exposes links that become affordances for later steps. The agent can use direct access, link-following traversal, or browse-and-aggregate strategies.

## Retrieval design

The strongest design point is the **contract between knowledge organization and agent control**. Search is only one operation; reading can reveal new traversal targets, and directory indices support overview-style browsing.

Termination is explicit: stop when reasoning chains are traced, the tool-call budget is exhausted, or consecutive empty searches exceed a patience threshold.

The separate Error Book runs at index/maintenance time. It records recurring structural and semantic compilation failures, turns them into reusable constraints, and drives deterministic plus periodic LLM repairs.

## Compared to what

The paper compares with Dense RAG, RAPTOR, GraphRAG, HippoRAG 2, LightRAG and related systems. Its appendix makes a useful distinction between **index product** and **agent traversal surface**: chunks, summaries, triples, and vectors are different from human-readable linked pages that expose explicit navigation paths.

## Evidence

The paper reports gains of **2.0–8.1 F1** over the strongest graph-based baselines on HotpotQA, MuSiQue, and 2WikiMultiHopQA, plus the best overall AuthTrace accuracy in its table.

The more important evidence is the ablation:

- keeping the compiled Wiki but disabling progressive traversal causes an **11.7–13.8 F1** drop;
- keeping compilation/traversal but disabling Error Book updates/repair causes a **3.4–4.0 F1** drop.

That gives unusually direct evidence that the runtime traversal policy is not merely free-riding on the richer structure.

## Why it matters

This corrects the radar's earlier chronology. Retrieval-interface redesign did **not** suddenly appear in August: LLM-Wiki made the agent-native environment argument explicitly in May, with a stronger structure-vs-traversal ablation than many newer papers.

So the August story should be “**convergence and sharper factorization**” rather than “the field just moved beyond top-k.”

## Limitations / questions

- The public multi-hop corpora use the union of context paragraphs from the first 500 examples, not full Wikipedia; web-scale traversal remains open.
- Compilation cost is front-loaded and the authors explicitly identify directory/index scalability as a problem beyond tens of thousands of pages.
- How much of the interface transfers when knowledge is rapidly changing, transactional, multimodal, or too large to compile into a coherent Wiki?

**Curator take:** **4/5**. A strong missing anchor because it makes knowledge organization and retrieval interface inseparable design objects and backs the traversal claim with a useful ablation.
