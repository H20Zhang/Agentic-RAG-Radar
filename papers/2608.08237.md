## Problem

Production RAG is usually tuned with one global passage budget. That makes easy queries pay unnecessary retrieval/reranking cost while hard queries may still need more evidence. Under a P95/P99 SLO, a single k creates a poor global compromise between answer quality and tail latency.

## Core idea

SAGE treats retrieval as constrained resource allocation. It runs a lightweight hybrid probe retrieval, extracts score/rank gaps and sparse–dense agreement features, and uses a RandomForest policy trained from offline budget sweeps to choose `k ∈ {2,3,5,7,10,15,20,25,30}`. A held-out calibration step tunes aggressiveness toward a target SLO.

## Agent loop

This is **not** a multi-round LLM agent loop:

`probe retrieval → retrieval features → choose k → assemble top-k → generate → monitor SLO`

Training uses an expensive offline oracle that sweeps all k values and supplies imitation labels; deployment uses only the learned policy.

## Retrieval design

The substrate is hybrid BM25 + BGE-M3 fused with RRF. The policy operates on retrieval-side features, adds no LLM call, and is reported at <1 ms inference overhead. The probe retrieval itself is reported at about 300 ms and is included in the retrieval path.

## Compared to what

The main experiments compare against static-k configurations, random-k, and component ablations. The paper discusses Adaptive-k and Stop-RAG as nearby work, but they are **not directly benchmarked** in the main baseline family. That makes the production-SLO formulation clearer than the adaptive-retrieval novelty claim.

## Evidence

On the 334-query Natural Questions latency test with a 5 s target:

- static `k=20`: 30% SLO compliance, 5.6 s P95, 24% EM, 100% normalized retrieval cost;
- SAGE: 95% SLO compliance, 3.6 s P95, 22% EM, average `k=9.8`, 49% normalized cost.

The paper also reports transfer of one NQ-trained policy to HotpotQA, UnSeenTimeQA, and several LLM families, compared with static `k=10`, while preserving reported EM.

## Why it matters

The durable idea is **budget as an action**. Agentic RAG evaluation should not treat calls/tokens/context volume only as after-the-fact metrics; the controller can actively allocate them per query.

But the research bar should now move immediately to **adaptive-vs-adaptive** comparison: SLO-aware learned allocation versus strong calibrated gating, Adaptive-k, or value-based stopping on the same substrate and hardware.

## Limitations / questions

- Does SAGE dominate prior adaptive-k / stopping controllers under the same SLO and retrieval substrate?
- Is 334 queries enough for stable P95 conclusions in a production-systems claim?
- How does the policy behave under workload drift, retriever upgrades, or bursty contention where latency is not a stationary function of k?

**Curator take:** **3/5**. A useful production control point and credible fixed-k result; importance is capped until adaptive baselines and workload-scale tail-latency evidence are stronger.
