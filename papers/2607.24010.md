# When Should Active RAG Retrieve? A Budget-Aware Evaluation of Utility, Calibration, and Cost

**arXiv:** [2607.24010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24010) · **Published:** 2026-07-27 · **Category:** Evaluation & Analysis · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High

**Tags:** `active RAG` `budget` `calibration` `retrieval harm` `cost` `evaluation`

> **TL;DR.** Active-RAG routers should be compared at matched **realized operating points**, not merely the same nominal retrieval rate: ranking quality, threshold transfer, retrieval harm, and trigger-side compute are distinct failure modes.

## Problem

A paper can say “50% retrieval budget” while its calibrated threshold actually retrieves on more or fewer than 50% of held-out queries. Accuracy then confounds policy quality with a looser operating point. The same nominal evidence-use rate can also hide different pre-decision costs.

## Core idea

Define per-example retrieval utility as the correctness change between retrieved-context and no-retrieval answers. Evaluate routers with three frontiers: an exact matched-usage frontier for ranking quality, a deployable calibrated-threshold frontier, and a conservative frontier that trades accuracy for fewer budget violations. Add retrieval-harm audits and router-specific cost decomposition.

## Agent loop / control flow

`routing score → calibrated threshold → retrieve or skip → answer → realized usage + utility/harm + cost audit`

## Retrieval design

The paper compares seven trigger scores across query complexity, generation uncertainty, BM25 evidence scores/margins, and lightweight learned utility routers. Main runs use candidate-paragraph BM25; noisier global BM25 and dense settings test whether conclusions survive evidence-source changes.

## Compared to what

This is not a new router claiming another accuracy win. Its closest comparison is **single-point Active RAG evaluation** that reports accuracy at a nominal target retrieval rate without verifying held-out usage, harm, or decision-side compute.

## Evidence

Retrieval is not monotonic: in the main Qwen2.5-1.5B runs it harms **8.8% of 2Wiki** examples and **4.0% of HotpotQA** examples even though always-retrieve improves aggregate accuracy. At a nominal 50% target, routers realize different held-out usage and violation rates; simple uncertainty or query-complexity triggers often overlap with or beat learned utility routers.

The ranking also changes with the evidence source and model family. On HotpotQA, switching from per-example candidate BM25 to global BM25 increases harm and changes the best exact-50 router. That makes “best trigger” less stable than the evaluation protocol itself.

## Why it matters

This paper upgrades a recurring radar caveat into a concrete evaluation contract. **Same nominal budget is not same realized budget**, and realized evidence use is still not total deployment cost when an uncertainty router generates once before deciding or a retrieval-score router pays a probe retrieval.

For future Agentic RAG comparisons, a quality–cost point without threshold-transfer and harm accounting should be treated as incomplete evidence.

## Limitations / questions

- The main experiments are controlled routing diagnostics over candidate evidence, not full open-domain search.
- The Adaptive-RAG-style baseline approximates query-complexity routing rather than reproducing the original pipeline end to end.
- Binary retrieve/skip is simpler than multi-tool agents with variable turns, k, context size, and stopping decisions.

**Curator take:** **4/5**. Important less as a new method than as an evaluation primitive: it makes the operating point itself auditable.
