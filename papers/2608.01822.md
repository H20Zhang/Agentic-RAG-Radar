> **TL;DR.** SearchMaster's important claim is not simply that self-play can train a search agent. ## Problem

Search-agent self-play sounds attractive because it can remove human-written questions and expert trajectories. But the generator can optimize the wrong thing. A task may look multi-hop while still being solvable from one page; success-rate difficulty can favor shallow lookup tasks; and answer reward can reinforce repeatedly opening documents instead of issuing targeted searches.

So the real problem is **self-generated supervision quality**, not raw synthetic-data volume.

## Core idea

One shared Qwen3.5-9B policy plays two roles in a local search environment:

- the **Proposer** explores documents and constructs a task;
- the **Solver** answers that task using search/open/find tools.

SearchMaster regulates this loop with three mechanisms. **Evidence-Chain Generation (ECG)** requires explicit cross-document evidence before question generation. **Search-Depth Reward (SDR)** scores difficulty using the minimum search depth among successful solver rollouts, so a task is not considered hard if any successful path solves it shallowly. **Over-Opening Penalty (OOP)** discourages the open/search ratio from drifting toward redundant reading. A frozen verifier checks task validity and answer correctness, and verified proposer/solver groups are optimized jointly with GRPO.

## Agent loop

`seed document → proposer builds evidence chain + task → filters/verifier → solver search rollouts → correctness/search-depth/opening rewards → GRPO update → repeat`

At deployment, the learned model acts as a search agent; the proposer/verifier machinery is training-time infrastructure.

## Retrieval design

The search environment itself is comparatively standard: `search`, `open`, and `find` over a local corpus. The research delta is in **how training examples are produced and selected**.

That matters because the task generator and reward function jointly define the policy's curriculum. If the generator produces shortcut questions or the reward prefers success without sufficient search, the learner can improve its objective while becoming a weaker research agent.

## Compared to what

The strongest causal baseline in the paper is its **naive same-pipeline self-play** condition, not the heterogeneous external leaderboard. Naive self-play keeps the same filters and solver verification but uses rollout success rate as task difficulty.

Prior Search Self-Play and Dr. Zero are the closest conceptual predecessors. SearchMaster's delta is to make evidence-chain grounding, minimum successful search depth, and tool-use balance explicit rather than relying on looser self-play signals.

## Evidence

On the same Qwen3.5-9B backbone, SearchMaster raises the reported average across six deep-search benchmarks from **38.19% to 51.52%**, including **30.12% to 60.24%** on BrowseComp-Plus.

The component evidence is more informative than the external leaderboard. On BrowseComp-Plus:

- naive self-play: **45.18%**;
- `+ ECG`: **53.25%**;
- `+ SDR`: **52.89%**;
- `+ ECG + SDR`: **57.71%**;
- full SearchMaster with OOP: **60.24%**.

The paper also reports that ECG increases true multi-hop task generation, SDR keeps the easiest successful search depth high as training proceeds, and OOP suppresses a drift toward redundant open calls.

## Why it matters

Agentic-search learning has mostly asked **which policy or retriever should be optimized**. SearchMaster adds another object: **which tasks and trajectories are allowed to become supervision**.

That is important for self-improving agents. A self-evolution loop can silently collapse toward easy or reward-hacking tasks even if optimization is stable. The training distribution needs its own grounding, difficulty, and behavior constraints.

## Limitations / questions

- Self-play remains expensive: many proposer/solver rollouts and verifier calls are required to retain a small number of high-quality seeds.
- The headline external comparison mixes model sizes, systems, and evaluation setups; the same-backbone naive-self-play and component ablations are much stronger causal evidence.
- Training happens in a large local corpus, while online transfer is evaluated under a specific long-context search scaffold. How robust is the learned behavior to different retrievers, harnesses, and live-web volatility?
- Does rewarding search depth risk optimizing for unnecessarily long evidence chains when a shorter but equally valid search policy exists?

**Curator take:** **4/5**. Important because it turns self-play **data-generation quality and curriculum** into a first-class optimization problem and provides useful same-backbone ablations. The hidden cost is that self-improvement still depends on expensive verification and rollout infrastructure.
