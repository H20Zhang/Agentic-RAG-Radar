# LENS: In-Context Search via Latent Evidence Exploration over Dynamic Raw Documents

**arXiv:** [2608.16185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16185) · **Published:** 2026-08-17 · **Updated:** 2026-08-18 · **Category:** Retrieval & Tool Use · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High

**Tags:** `documents` `iterative search` `budget allocation` `adaptive stopping` `open-domain QA`

> **TL;DR.** LENS moves evidence materialization from indexing time to query time. It treats raw-document regions as a latent evidence space and sequentially proposes, scores, and refines regions under a budget. The strongest result is **fresh evidence localization and grounding**, not better answer EM than ReAct; the price is more online work.

## Problem

Fixed chunking and persistent indexes decide evidence boundaries before the query exists. That is efficient for stable lookup, but it can become stale after raw files change and can choose the wrong granularity for a later question.

The opposite extreme—search raw documents directly at query time—preserves freshness but needs a way to avoid unconstrained scanning.

## Core idea

LENS formulates **Budgeted Evidence Localization** over a latent evidence space of query-dependent raw-document regions.

A low-cost prior proposes candidates from lexical, local, structural, and reusable evidence cues. An LLM relevance oracle inspects raw text, updates per-fact beliefs, adapts proposal weights, and stops when the evidence requirements are resolved or the budget is exhausted. The final evidence is consolidated into source-grounded regions.

## Agent loop

`build query-conditioned prior → propose raw-document regions → inspect relevance → update beliefs/proposal mix → continue or stop under budget → consolidate grounded evidence → answer`

## Retrieval design

LENS does not require a persistent retrieval index for the queried raw collection. Evidence boundaries are induced after the query rather than fixed during preprocessing.

This makes **materialization placement** a first-class systems variable: pay offline to build stable chunks/indexes, or pay online to localize evidence from the raw source after the information need is known.

## Compared to what

Fresh and stale indexed RAG, an index-free ReAct-style baseline over matched corpus snapshots, a closed-book reference, and component ablations that remove the multi-signal prior or sequential exploration.

## Evidence

On the controlled 500-question setting, LENS reports **62.4% EM / 84.8% evidence recall**, versus **65.2% EM / 50.4% evidence recall** for ReAct-style search. That is a useful negative: the answer metric does **not** favor LENS, while evidence localization does.

On a fixed 150-question fullwiki raw-dump subset with zero indexing, answer EM is nearly tied (**43.3% LENS vs 42.7% ReAct**) while grounded answers are higher for LENS (**84.0% vs 70.7%**). Stale-index experiments show the expected freshness advantage after corpus changes.

The ablation also matters: removing sequential exploration materially hurts the fullwiki result, while removing the multi-signal prior does not reduce EM in that reported setting. The main contribution is therefore the adaptive localization loop more than every prior heuristic.

## Why it matters

LENS extends the radar's current `SIRA ↔ ReFind` question. Adaptivity has a location, but **evidence materialization has a location too**.

SIRA spends more intelligence before retrieval; ReFind preserves raw chat and adapts at query time; LENS pushes the same question to dynamic raw documents and query-conditioned evidence boundaries. The clean systems comparison is not “index-free wins,” but:

**offline freshness/structure cost ↔ online localization/LLM cost ↔ evidence fidelity**.

## Limitations / questions

- ReAct is better on D500 answer EM and essentially tied on fullwiki, so LENS is not an answer-quality win.
- The reported controlled accounting gives LENS more online tokens and latency than ReAct; zero indexing is not zero cost.
- Closed-book performance remains substantial on fullwiki, so final-answer attribution includes parametric knowledge.
- The evidence-localization gains still need transfer tests across more document formats, update patterns, and non-text substrates.

**Curator take:** **4/5**. Important because it makes query-time evidence materialization a concrete design point and keeps the negative answer-quality result visible. The decisive experiment is lifecycle-matched cost: same raw corpus/update stream, same model, and equal offline+online compute across indexed, direct-interaction, and latent-localization designs.
