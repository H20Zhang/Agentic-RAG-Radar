# GTA-RAG: Graph-Trajectory-Augmented Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Turn Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning

*Published 23 Aug 2026 · Learning & Optimization · Importance 4/5 · Full-text reviewed · Confidence medium*

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22479) · [Code](https://github.com/cjcj46262/GTA-RAG)

> **TL;DR.** GTA-RAG converts graph paths into retriever-validated target trajectories before natural-QA RL. Same-package ablations improve chain coverage and QA, but the headline package also changes graph construction, retrieval interface, data, and training.

| 30-second verdict | |
|---|---|
| **Why this paper matters** | It makes acquiring a complete evidence chain an explicit learning target. |
| **Strongest evidence** | Full versus no trajectory reward gives **74.1 vs 58.7** full-chain coverage and **49.7 vs 46.2** EM. |
| **Biggest caveat** | The held-out chain test is generated from the same graph-path process; lifecycle cost and matched external baselines are missing. |

## Research question

Can target-document trajectories teach multi-turn retrieval behavior that answer-only reward fails to specify?

## Research delta

The delta is not graph retrieval or GRPO alone. GTA-RAG samples paths from an offline OpenIE graph, verifies that the deployed retriever can reach their documents, and rewards acquisition of those targets before optimizing natural-question answer EM.

## Mechanism

`graph path → retriever validation → synthetic target trajectory → trajectory reward → natural-QA reward → inference search loop`

At inference, the agent searches a PPR+dense union interface. That substrate is part of the learned behavior.

## Evidence & attribution

| Claim | Evidence | Closest control | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target supervision improves chain acquisition | 74.1 vs 58.7 full-chain | same Qwen2.5-7B package, no trajectory reward | Supported |
| It improves downstream QA | 49.7/59.4 vs 46.2/56.0 EM/F1 | same ablation | Supported inside package |
| It learns general search policy | held-out target coverage 82.3 vs 68.1 | graph-synthetic targets | **Unproven** |
| It beats prior policy designs causally | larger main-table gains | unmatched interfaces/training | **Not attributable** |

The synthetic distribution is dominated by two-hop paths. Offline OpenIE construction, external generation, online tokens/latency, graph memory, and dollars are not closed.

## Where it fits

`answer-only RL → retriever-validated evidence-chain supervision`

This is a package-level early signal for **retriever-validated evidence-chain supervision**. It complements trajectory learning but is not yet a general retrieval-policy result.

## Open question

Would the same target reward improve natural, unseen evidence chains when graph, retriever, interface, rollout count, and compute are held fixed—and when four-plus-hop examples are not vanishingly rare?
