# Retrieval, Reward, and Training Protocols: What Matters in Training Search Agents?

**arXiv:** [2605.27881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27881) · **Published:** 2026-05-27 · **Updated:** 2026-05-29 · **Category:** Evaluation & Analysis · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High

**Code:** https://github.com/YiboZhao624/SearchAgentReview

**Tags:** `reinforcement learning` `budget allocation` `iterative search` `open-domain QA`

> **TL;DR.** This is a controlled-baseline paper, not another search-agent algorithm. Its strongest result is that **retrieval environment quality can dominate algorithm choice**: missing evidence in the standard Wikipedia corpus creates spurious rewards, while process-level credit assignment often changes search style rather than reliably improving final accuracy.


## Problem

Search-agent RL results are difficult to compare because papers vary several things at once: corpus coverage, tool-call interface, reward design, training data, off-policy degree, and search budget. A more complex RL method can therefore appear better because it is trained in a better environment—or worse because its process reward pushes the wrong behavior.

## Core idea

Build a unified experimental setup and vary three groups of factors independently:

1. **Retrieval environment:** audit and repair missing answer-bearing passages in the widely reused Wikipedia-2018 corpus.
2. **Reward / credit assignment:** compare simple final-answer outcome reward with GiGPO, IGPO, and Tree-GRPO-style process signals.
3. **Training protocol:** vary data diversity, rollout freshness/off-policy batch size, and train/eval search budgets.

## Agent loop

`reason about missing information → search → observe passages → update history → search again or answer`

The paper's contribution is to perturb the environment and training signals around this loop while keeping the basic action space comparable.

## Retrieval design

The retrieval corpus itself is treated as a causal variable. The authors find that the common Wiki-18 setup is missing answer-bearing documents for many training questions. In such cases a model can still emit the right answer from parametric knowledge, receive positive outcome reward, and be reinforced despite retrieval having failed.

That is a deeper confound than ordinary retriever recall: the **reward can certify a trajectory whose external evidence channel was impossible**.

## Compared to what

Search-R1's simple outcome reward versus GiGPO, IGPO, and Tree-GRPO; Wiki-18 versus the corrected Wiki-fixed corpus; Hermes versus Search-R1/XML tool-call formats; different rollout batch sizes / off-policy degrees; and different maximum search-turn budgets.

## Evidence

The corpus audit identifies **3,321 training questions** that are unanswerable from the incomplete retrieval corpus. For Search-R1, the model nevertheless produces the correct answer at least once on **1,697** of them, showing how parametric knowledge can create a positive reward despite failed evidence retrieval.

Under controlled reward comparisons, the simple Search-R1 outcome reward achieves the highest average EM in **three of five** reported model/format settings. Process-reward methods produce qualitatively different failure modes: in the process audit, **three of four trained methods reduce per-query supporting-document recall** relative to their untrained counterparts, while different methods trade fewer searches, more redundancy, or broader exploration.

Budget scaling is another negative result: in the Qwen3-8B setting, moving from a **4-turn train/eval budget to 32/32** lowers performance by roughly five points for Search-R1, GiGPO, and IGPO; giving more evaluation turns than the training budget adds at most about **0.3 points**. Longer search is not a free capability gain.

## Why it matters

For this radar, the paper makes the training causal model stricter:

`retrieval environment coverage × tool interface × reward/credit assignment × rollout freshness × search budget × base model`

This should not become six new taxonomy axes. The useful correction is simpler: **the retrieval/training environment is part of the algorithmic claim**. If the corpus lacks evidence, the tool syntax mismatches the model, or training budget induces different behavior, comparing RL objective names is not scientifically clean.

It also weakens a common intuition that finer process rewards must be better. A denser signal can over-specify behavior, suppress useful exploration, or reward redundant search.

## Limitations / questions

- The study focuses on local Wikipedia QA and 7B/8B-scale Qwen families; live-web search and frontier-agent behavior may differ.
- The corrected corpus solves a specific coverage problem, not general corpus drift or temporal freshness.
- Supporting-document process metrics require gold evidence annotations and are diagnostic rather than deployment-time signals.
- The work is methodological; it does not establish that simple outcome rewards are universally best, only that they remain a stronger baseline than many papers assume.

**Curator take:** **4/5**. Important because it changes what counts as a controlled learning result. The biggest implication is uncomfortable but useful: before inventing a better search-agent RL objective, verify that the retrieval environment can actually support the reward signal you are optimizing.
