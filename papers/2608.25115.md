# Less can be More: Relieving RAG Bottlenecks via Evidence Frontloading and Pressure-Adaptive Budgeting

*Published 25 Aug 2026 · Retrieval & Tool Use · Importance 4/5 · Full-text reviewed · Confidence medium*

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25115)

> **TL;DR.** PACE couples evidence frontloading inside a fixed retrieved pool with a serving-pressure controller that changes reranking depth as the reranker versus LLM bottleneck shifts. The paper supports a two-factor result—ordering preserves evidence at small budgets, pressure control reduces queueing—not a generic “adaptive RAG is better” claim.

## Research question

When reranking rather than generation becomes the serving bottleneck, can a RAG controller reduce reranking depth without discarding the multi-hop evidence needed downstream?

## Research delta

PACE separates two decisions that are easy to conflate:

`fixed retrieval pool → evidence-frontloading order → serving-pressure budget D(q) → reranker → top-5 context`

Evidence frontloading changes **which candidates appear early**. Pressure-adaptive budgeting changes **how many candidates are reranked**. The controller does not issue another retrieval query or expand the corpus boundary.

## Mechanism

The retriever first returns up to `Dmax` candidates. PACE greedily reorders them with a monotone-submodular marginal coverage objective that combines query relevance, complementarity, and soft evidence-chain anchors. A separate online controller observes relative reranker and LLM queue/service pressure and reduces or increases reranking budget `D` within configured bounds.

In the online comparison, the same pressure-adaptive budgeting policy is applied to all reordering methods. This is important: latency differences then mostly come from the budget controller, while recall differences at comparable selected `D` reveal evidence-ordering quality.

## Evidence & attribution

| Claim | Evidence | Closest control | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontloading preserves more complete evidence at smaller budgets | HotpotQA R@5 **41.13** at standard `D=100` vs PACE **62.38** at `D=50`; 2Wiki **48.76** vs **54.39** | Fixed-budget standard dense / reordering controls | Strong ordering result |
| Pressure-aware budgeting relieves reranker queueing | Under rising QPS, PACE reduces `D` and stabilizes p95 latency | Fixed `D=100` | System-specific but direct |
| Better ordering, not just a larger budget, drives online recall | Same adaptive-budget policy across reordering methods; PACE has higher R@5 at comparable `D` | Same budget controller | Useful factor isolation |
| Less reranking can beat full-budget top-5 recall | QPS 1.8: about half the budget, ~20% higher R@5 | Fixed `D=100` | Evidence-recall result, not answer-quality result |

## Where it fits

`resource accounting → serving-pressure-aware retrieval budget → evidence materialization`

This is an `early_signal`. It argues that runtime systems state belongs in the adaptivity surface, but it does not show that queue-pressure control itself improves semantic correctness.

## Open question

Keep the same retriever, candidate pool, reranker, generator, hardware, and arrival trace. Cross the two factors independently—`frontloading on/off × adaptive D on/off`—and report answer accuracy, evidence recall, p95 latency, realized reranker pairs, GPU-seconds, and failure cases where lower `D` hides necessary evidence. That would make the semantic-versus-serving trade explicit.
