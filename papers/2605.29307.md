# GrepSeek: Training Search Agents for Direct Corpus Interaction

**arXiv:** [2605.29307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29307) · **Published:** 2026-05-28 · **Category:** Learning & Optimization · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High

> **TL;DR.** GrepSeek asks whether DCI can be **learned by a compact model**, not merely improvised by a frontier coding agent. It combines verified cold-start trajectories, GRPO, and a semantics-preserving sharded shell executor.

## Problem

Raw DCI exposes a useful action space, but its success can depend on a very capable model discovering good search commands online. Direct RL in a huge corpus is unstable, and sequential shell scans add systems overhead.

## Core idea

Generate verified search trajectories with an answer-aware tutor and answer-blind planner, initialize a compact DCI policy with SFT, refine it with GRPO, and parallelize shell search across shards while preserving byte-exact command semantics.

## Agent loop

`reason → shell search/filter → inspect matches → refine lexical constraints → compose evidence → answer`

## Retrieval design

The corpus remains the environment rather than a ranked top-k API; the learned object is the policy that chooses exact lexical/file operations.

## Compared to what

The closest baselines are Search-R1-style agents backed by E5 or Qwen3 dense retrievers. The paper also ablates SFT and GRPO to test the training pipeline.

## Evidence

Across seven QA benchmarks GrepSeek reports **0.5691 micro-average F1**, above the strongest Search-R1 dense configuration at **0.5441**. Removing GRPO or SFT substantially lowers the average. The sharded executor reduces average shell-search latency from **5.39s to 0.71s**, up to **7.6×**.

The negative result matters: on **PopQA**, GrepSeek is significantly worse than the best dense baseline. Exact lexical matching misses aliases/diacritics and has no semantic relevance ranking, defining a real semantic tail where embeddings remain useful.

## Why it matters

The paper separates two questions that DCI initially bundled: **is the interface useful?** and **can a policy over that interface be trained efficiently?** It also makes dense retrieval look complementary rather than obsolete.

## Limitations / questions

- The end-to-end gain bundles trajectory synthesis, SFT, RL, and execution-engine improvements.
- Lexical DCI remains brittle under surface-form variation and overloaded keywords.
- Transfer beyond file/text corpora to web, SQL, APIs, or multimodal evidence remains open.

**Curator take:** **4/5.** Important because it turns DCI into a learnable policy class and preserves a strong negative result showing where lexical interaction breaks.
