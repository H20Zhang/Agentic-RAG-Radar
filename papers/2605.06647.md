# Superintelligent Retrieval Agent: The Next Frontier of Information Retrieval

**arXiv:** [2605.06647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06647) · **Published:** 2026-05-07 · **Updated:** 2026-06-04 · **Category:** Retrieval & Tool Use · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High

**Code:** https://github.com/facebookresearch/sira

**Tags:** `sparse` `query rewrite` `planning` `training free` `open-domain QA`

> **TL;DR.** SIRA relocates adaptivity from **after retrieval** to **before retrieval**. Instead of learning corpus vocabulary by repeatedly reading snippets, an LLM predicts missing evidence vocabulary, validates it with index-visible corpus statistics, and compiles a discriminative weighted BM25 action. The strongest lesson is not “one shot beats agents”; it is that the number of search rounds is meaningless unless we also ask what retrieval controls and corpus signals are exposed before the first result is read.

## Problem

Multi-round search agents often improve later queries using vocabulary, entities, and near misses surfaced by earlier retrieval calls. That makes interaction useful, but also means the controller is partly learning how the corpus is organized through expensive trial and error.

SIRA asks whether some of that exploration can be compiled away if the retrieval engine exposes **corpus-discriminative signals before evidence retrieval**.

## Core idea

A frozen LLM produces an expected-response sketch: concepts and terms likely to distinguish answer-bearing documents. Candidate terms are grounded against the target index using document frequency, removing absent or overly common vocabulary. The surviving terms become weighted lexical controls in BM25.

The main method also performs corpus-side LLM enrichment offline, anticipating search vocabulary missing from each document and injecting validated n-grams into the index. In the strict BEIR setting, the online path is one reasoning step plus optional corpus-stat checks and one final BM25 call, with no retrieved passages used for reformulation.

For BrowseComp-Wikipedia the authors deliberately remove the expensive document-enrichment pass and use Wikipedia categories as grounded corpus-visible features instead.

## Agent loop

Strict setting: `predict evidence vocabulary → validate with corpus statistics → compile weighted BM25 action → retrieve`

The key absence is deliberate: **no `retrieve → read snippets → reformulate` loop before the final retrieval action**.

## Retrieval design

SIRA treats the inverted index itself as an agent-facing information source. Document-frequency / IDF signals answer a different question from passage retrieval: not “what is the answer?”, but “will this proposed term actually discriminate inside this corpus?”

This distinction matters. A plausible LLM-generated expansion can be useless if it is absent from the index or common across confusers; lightweight corpus statistics can reject it before spending evidence budget.

## Compared to what

Standard BM25, E5, SPLADE, Doc2Query, HyDE, chain-of-thought expansion, Search-R1, GrepRAG, ShellAgent, and multi-round Perplexity search on the new BrowseComp-Wikipedia setting.

## Evidence

Across ten BEIR datasets, SIRA reports **0.6908 Recall@10 / 0.5723 NDCG@10** on average, versus E5 at **0.6478 / 0.5434**, SPLADE at **0.6253 / 0.5223**, Search-R1(E5) at **0.6161 / 0.5216**, and BM25 at **0.5302 / 0.4247**. HyDE, CoT, GrepRAG, ShellAgent, and SIRA share the same frozen Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-FP8 backbone; Search-R1 does not.

On BrowseComp-Wikipedia, the strongest Claude 4.6 Opus SIRA configuration reports **9.70 / 15.27 / 36.14 Recall@1/10/100**, versus the Claude Perplexity agent at **2.59 / 4.74 / 32.33**.

A negative that should not be buried: **with GPT-5.4**, SIRA reports **5.71 / 13.13 / 18.51**, while Perplexity reports **3.02 / 6.90 / 31.47**. SIRA wins at the tight budgets but loses badly at Recall@100 for that backbone. So the evidence does not support a blanket “compiled one-shot retrieval dominates multi-round search at every budget/model” claim.

## Why it matters

SIRA creates a useful counterpoint to DCI-style and ReFind-style interaction. Those works argue that richer runtime operations can repair lossy retrieval surfaces. SIRA asks the inverse: **can a better programmable retrieval surface remove the need for some runtime exploration?**

The emerging research variable is therefore not `number of rounds`. It is:

`what corpus knowledge is observable before retrieval × what retrieval action can be compiled × what new information becomes available only after seeing results`.

If the relevant discriminative vocabulary can be predicted and grounded before retrieval, additional rounds may be wasted. If the next query depends on a newly discovered entity, timestamp, or relationship, result-conditioned interaction can still be essential.

## Limitations / questions

- The main BEIR system performs expensive LLM enrichment over every corpus document. This is a large offline systems cost even though online search is cheap.
- The approach assumes the frozen LLM has useful semantic/domain priors; genuinely private or obscure corpora may weaken that assumption.
- Search-R1 is not a same-backbone/controller comparison.
- BrowseComp-Wikipedia gives SIRA access to grounded Wikipedia categories, so the comparison to a production Perplexity search tool bundles interface structure with policy.
- The Wikipedia protocol is evidence-blind but not always literally one BM25 call; multiple category-grounded calls may be issued under the fixed page budget.

**Curator take:** **4/5**. Important because it falsifies a lazy trend narrative: more retrieval rounds are not themselves intelligence. But its strongest result also depends on corpus-side structure/enrichment, so the decisive future comparison is compiled pre-retrieval control versus result-conditioned interaction under the **same corpus observability, backend, and total compute**.
