# Agentic RAG Yearly — 2026

> **Coverage:** 2026-01-01 to 2026-08-16 · **Status:** rolling · **Scope note:** this is a year-to-date map over papers currently curated by the radar, not a claim of complete historical coverage before backfill matures.

## Year-to-date thesis

**The durable 2026 shift is from “retriever + agent” toward explicit design of the agent's information environment: where the corpus boundary sits, what a backend surfaces, which sources/states are admissible, what evidence operations remain visible, what external and internal state persists, what policy controls interaction, what supervision shaped it, and what resources are actually spent.**

Backfill keeps raising the causal bar. DCI/RISE/DR-DCI/RARG expose interaction-space design; Pi-Serini shows that a conventional BM25 baseline can be badly understated by weak tuning, shallow surfaced rankings, or limited browse/read affordances; SIEVE separates admissibility, ranking, inspection, and reading; SGR-Bench makes **external retrieval state** explicit; SearchMaster makes training-distribution quality explicit.

The methodological consequence is stricter: a serious comparison now needs **backend/interface calibration, environment-state accounting, harness matching, adaptive baselines, realized-resource accounting, and supervision-distribution accounting**, plus a historical prior-art baseline when the control-loop shape is old.

## 1. Retrieval becomes an interaction-space and interface-factorization problem

A-RAG exposes explicit retrieval operations. DCI makes the stronger move: bypass fixed similarity retrieval and expose raw corpus operations, introducing **retrieval-interface resolution** as a design lens. RISE restores a scalable boundary while preserving a persistent interaction space; DR-DCI makes that boundary dynamically expandable; RARG carries relevance into local execution.

The durable progression is:

`top-k evidence delivery → raw high-resolution interaction → bounded/dynamic workspace → relevance-guided interaction`

Pi-Serini prevents this lineage from being misread as “retrievers are obsolete.” On BrowseComp-Plus, tuned BM25 plus a deep cached ranking and browse/read tools is already a very strong agentic-search substrate. Its most useful negative is that **surfaced recall keeps rising while previewed recall saturates**: a backend can retrieve relevant evidence the agent never actually inspects.

SIEVE adds another dimension inside the environment:

`candidate admissibility → ranking → inspection → selective read`

The durable lesson is therefore not dense versus lexical, nor retriever versus grep. It is: **where does relevance constrain the environment, how much candidate information survives that constraint, and what operations remain for inspecting/refining evidence?**

## 2. State has multiple layers and multiple owners

S2G-RAG makes sufficiency/missing-information state explicit. DocNavRAG couples evidence state to document-native navigation. LoongReflect adds rollback/recovery over active reasoning state. DR-DCI highlights durable external workspace state that can survive reasoning-context reset.

SGR-Bench adds a separate axis: **environment retrieval state**. On specialized data sites, finding the correct source does not expose the correct evidence unless filters, views, hierarchies, scopes, or time windows are configured correctly. Its audited failures are dominated by retrieval-scope drift and criterion mismatch rather than final answer composition.

The year-end map should preserve these distinctions:

`external source state ≠ persistent evidence workspace ≠ internal evidence/reasoning state`

A generic “memory” label obscures the intervention point.

## 3. Learning moves across multiple control boundaries—and into curriculum design

Agentic-R changes retriever utility from static similarity toward trajectory-level downstream value. Critic-R turns reasoning feedback into query repair and retriever supervision. GrepSeek learns policy over a direct-corpus interface. SPARKLE/Graph-R1 learn sequential control; SAGE learns per-query resource allocation; LoongReflect learns recovery.

SearchMaster adds a distinct object: **which self-generated tasks and trajectories become training signal**. Its same-backbone naive-self-play comparison and component ablations support the claim that task grounding, difficulty semantics, and opening regularization matter, while leaving verifier/rollout cost as part of the training system.

Thus “learned Agentic RAG” is not one research object. A paper should identify whether it learned ranking, query refinement, corpus operations, state recovery, budget allocation, or the task/trajectory distribution itself—and compare against a controller with the same environment/state/action space.

## 4. Harness, backend calibration, and evidence delivery become causal variables

Is Grep All You Need? shows that retriever rankings can change materially with the agent harness and how tool output is delivered. Pi-Serini adds a complementary baseline warning: **same label, different backend configuration/depth/interface is not the same retriever condition**. Default or shallow BM25 can be an artificially weak lexical baseline.

These are not arguments that one harness or one retriever family is “best.” They mean those choices belong inside the experimental model, not in the implementation appendix.

The current causal lens is:

`substrate × corpus boundary/interface resolution × environment retrieval state × harness/delivery × agent state × policy × realized resources × base model × training distribution × historical baseline`

Pi-Serini's tuning/depth are normally subfactors of interface/resource matching rather than additional top-level axes; the purpose of the lens is causal discipline, not taxonomy inflation.

## 5. Resource budget becomes both an action and an operating point

Know Before You Fetch separates calls, passage volume, and latency. SAGE makes k a learned per-query action. When Should Active RAG Retrieve? makes held-out usage, retrieval harm, and controller-side cost explicit.

The interaction-space lineage adds workspace size and local tool I/O. Pi-Serini adds **surfaced ranking depth versus inspected depth**: deeper retrieval can improve backend recall while most candidates remain unread. SIEVE adds selective reading granularity. SearchMaster adds training-side proposer/solver/verifier compute.

A smaller prompt is not necessarily cheaper search; fewer tool calls are not necessarily lower wall time; deeper rankings are not useful evidence unless the controller exploits them.

## 6. Novelty and composition get harder tests

Forgotten History or Test-of-Time? pushes retrieve→verify→reformulate baselines back to classical IR/QA. SGR-Bench pushes source discovery into state-gated data access. VAKRA pushes evaluation into executable cross-source composition across APIs and documents.

Together they constrain three stages of a claim: show what is genuinely new compared with older control ideas; show that the system can establish the right external evidence state; then show that identity/evidence/policy remain coherent across heterogeneous sources.

## What has weakened

**“Agentic = iterative retrieval”** is no longer meaningful: the loop is old and the design space is much more specific.

**“Better retriever = better information access”** is too weak. DCI shows evidence resolution and operations can dominate after relevant documents are reachable; Pi-Serini shows the converse, that an allegedly weak retriever may simply be poorly tuned/shallow or badly exposed; SIEVE shows admissibility, ranking, inspection, and reading are separable.

**“Found the right source = retrieval succeeded”** is now also too weak. SGR-Bench shows the source can still be in the wrong retrieval state.

**“More self-play = better self-improvement”** is too weak. SearchMaster shows task grounding, difficulty semantics, and tool-use regularization can dominate the generated supervision.

**“Same model + different retriever = controlled”** is insufficient if backend tuning/depth, harness/tool-delivery semantics, or environment state differ.

## Open problems entering the rest of 2026

1. **Adaptive boundary + environment state:** jointly decide workspace size, admissibility relaxation, and source-specific filter/scope state under explicit latency/I/O/context budgets.
2. **Semantic-symbolic co-design:** use learned relevance to guide exact corpus operations and structured eligibility without reintroducing a hard top-k bottleneck.
3. **Layered state semantics:** separate external source state, persistent evidence workspace, compacted memory/provenance, and reversible reasoning state.
4. **Backend × interface × harness causal benchmarks:** independently swap backend tuning/depth, corpus boundary, inspection/read affordances, evidence delivery, controller, and model on the same executable tasks.
5. **Self-improvement without curriculum collapse:** measure task shortcut rate, search-depth drift, verifier dependence, and rollout cost as the learner co-evolves with its generated training distribution.
6. **Cross-source generalization:** test document-derived interface/state ideas on web, APIs, SQL, graphs, code, and multimodal data.
7. **Counterfactual trajectory repair:** patch backend exposure, inspection choice, environment state, or controller action one at a time and measure causal recovery.

## Rolling status

The year-end version should preserve only shifts that survive matched backend/interface/harness conditions, explicit environment-state accounting, strong adaptive baselines, realized-resource accounting, supervision-distribution checks, and broader workloads. The current backfill is a reminder that both chronology and baseline quality are part of research quality: later systems should not be credited for abstractions already explicit earlier, and richer-agent claims should not be built on deliberately weak conventional retrieval baselines.
