# Agentic RAG Yearly — 2026

> **Coverage:** 2026-01-01 to 2026-08-12 · **Status:** rolling · **Scope note:** this is a year-to-date map over papers currently curated by the radar, not a claim of complete historical coverage before backfill matures.

## Year-to-date thesis

**The durable 2026 shift is not simply “more agentic retrieval.” It is the emergence of an explicit information-acquisition control stack whose components can be discussed separately: environment/interface, evidence state, policy/stopping, and resource allocation.**

The second durable lesson is methodological: the comparison baseline must expand in two directions at once. Causal claims need matched substrate/operations/state/budget; novelty claims need a deeper IR/QA history than recent RAG papers alone.

## Durable shifts so far

### 1. Retrieval becomes an agent-facing environment

A-RAG exposes explicit keyword/semantic/read operations. LLM-Wiki goes further by compiling documents into linked, inspectable Wiki pages whose structure is directly traversable. READ and DocNavRAG sharpen the interface argument for structured documents.

**Old default → new design point:** one opaque `top-k(query)` primitive → a small composable information-access surface.

**Best current evidence:** LLM-Wiki reports an 11.7–13.8 F1 drop when progressive traversal is removed while keeping the compiled Wiki. That is unusually useful evidence that runtime control contributes beyond substrate quality.

**Counter-evidence:** READ reports BM25 statistically indistinguishable from READ in its setting. Sometimes a stronger primitive may explain the gain without sophisticated agent control.

Together these results make the right question **interface × policy**, not interface *or* policy.

### 2. Evidence progress becomes explicit state

Search-o1 moved retrieval into the reasoning trajectory. DocNavRAG makes the next abstraction clearer: represent collected and missing evidence explicitly rather than relying only on raw prompt/reasoning history. ACE-GraphRAG similarly treats context gaps as a control signal for adaptive context construction.

**Old default → new design point:** implicit trajectory history → inspectable evidence/progress state.

**Open risk:** there is still limited matched-budget evidence that explicit state itself, rather than extra retrieval opportunities, causes the improvement.

### 3. Retrieval budget becomes a controller action

Know Before You Fetch and SAGE make `how much retrieval?` a first-class decision rather than a globally tuned constant.

Know Before You Fetch contributes the cleaner accounting vocabulary: retrieval-call rate, full-context rate, passage budget, and measured latency are not interchangeable. Its negative result—that a compact `k=1` action can save passages without saving a retrieval call, and that a confidence probe can make gating slower in some model regimes—is likely more durable than any single frontier number.

SAGE adds a learned SLO-aware policy over retrieval-side features and shows a strong fixed-k latency/quality frontier improvement. The open question is adaptive-vs-adaptive comparison, since its main baseline family is static k.

### 4. Policy learning moves from local relevance toward system utility

Graph-R1 learns multi-turn graph interaction, Agentic-R trains retrieval for trajectory-level downstream utility, and SAGE learns resource allocation from an offline latency–quality oracle.

The common change is objective scope:

`local similarity → action/trajectory/resource utility`

Credit assignment remains the bottleneck. A learned policy is not automatically the causal source of a system-level gain when the environment and resource budget change at the same time.

### 5. The novelty baseline gets older

Forgotten History or Test-of-Time? reconstructs classical IR/QA antecedents for query refinement, answer verification, and closed-loop retrieval, with QUALIFIER as a concrete retrieve→verify→relax/reformulate example.

This does **not** collapse modern Agentic RAG into old QA. It changes what has to be claimed as new. The interesting novelty may be LLM-scale state representation, richer tool interfaces, learned control, generalization, or interaction with new substrates—not merely an iterative loop.

## Papers that currently define the 2026 map

1. **A-RAG** — clean early interface abstraction.
2. **LLM-Wiki** — compiled agent-facing environment plus strong traversal ablation.
3. **Agentic-R** — retrieval objective moves toward trajectory utility.
4. **Know Before You Fetch** — calibrated budget allocation with unusually disciplined cost accounting.
5. **READ / Beyond Top-K** — strong warning that primitive quality can dominate apparent agentic gains.
6. **DocNavRAG** — environment + explicit evidence-state co-design.
7. **Forgotten History or Test-of-Time?** — raises the novelty baseline beyond the LLM era.

ACE-GraphRAG, Search-o1, Graph-R1, PlanRAG, SAGE, and the Agentic RAG SoK remain important supporting design points.

## The evaluation standard 2026 is forcing

The current lens is:

`substrate × operation set × state × policy × budget × base model × historical baseline`

A credible **causal** claim should control the first six as much as possible. A credible **novelty** claim should explain the seventh.

That means more:

- same-interface fixed/heuristic/agentic policy comparisons;
- adaptive-vs-adaptive resource-control baselines;
- separate calls/tokens/passages/latency/controller-overhead reporting;
- explicit-state ablations;
- classical IR/QA re-implementations when the claimed mechanism has obvious historical antecedents.

## What has weakened

**“Agentic = iterative retrieval” is no longer a useful research claim.** Both modern 2026 work and classical IR history make that too broad.

**“Less retrieved context = lower cost” is also too weak.** Probe generation, retrieval-call count, passage volume, reranking, and tail latency can move differently.

**“A richer graph/index proves the policy works” is increasingly untenable.** LLM-Wiki and READ, in different ways, show why substrate/interface and adaptive control need separate tests.

## Open problems entering the rest of 2026

1. **Minimal sufficient retrieval API:** expressive enough for complex evidence needs but small enough for reliable control.
2. **Explicit information state:** stable representations of evidence coverage, uncertainty, provenance, and missing requirements.
3. **Adaptive-vs-adaptive evaluation:** compare serious controllers rather than static-k straw baselines.
4. **Resource-aware objectives:** train against calls/tokens/latency/cost rather than report them only after the fact.
5. **Transfer across substrates:** documents, web, SQL, code, graphs, and multimodal evidence.
6. **Historical baseline discipline:** distinguish new capability from old control-loop shape implemented with new models.

## Rolling status

The current thesis should become harder to change as the year progresses, but not protected from falsification. The year-end version should preserve only shifts that survive broader workloads and stronger matched comparisons, and should explicitly list ideas that looked important mid-year but failed to generalize.
