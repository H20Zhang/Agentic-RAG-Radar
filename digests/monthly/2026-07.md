# Agentic RAG Monthly — 2026-07

> **Coverage:** 2026-07-01 to 2026-07-31 · **Status:** finalized · **Timing basis:** original paper publication date; legacy Radar acceptance timestamps are unavailable

## Month thesis

**July contributes two complementary signals about retrieval control:** When Should Active RAG Retrieve? studies the retrieve/skip operating point; RARG++ studies relevance-guided execution after retrieval is available. Two papers do not establish a field trend, and their gains concern different intervention points.

## 1. Retrieve/skip policies need operating-point audits

[When Should Active RAG Retrieve?](../../papers/2607.24010.md) shows that retrieval is sometimes harmful: 8.8% of its 2Wiki cases and 4.0% of HotpotQA cases. The best router also varies across tasks. This makes threshold transfer, utility ranking, and trigger-side cost part of the method claim rather than tuning details.

The causal limit is important: a better final score can come from choosing a different operating point, not from a universally better uncertainty signal.

## 2. Relevance can guide execution, but cost has multiple dimensions

[RARG++](../../papers/2607.24223.md) carries relevance into a corpus-interaction loop. On BrowseComp-Plus it reports 84% with 23.9 tools, compared with 78%/28.7 for RISE and 78%/99.1 for DCI. At 1M scale it reports 79 versus 69 for RISE-BM25.

Its generative reranker is the useful negative: 17.8 tools but 75% accuracy. Fewer interactions do not automatically mean a better policy or lower total cost, because reranking compute, context, latency, and quality move separately.

## Causal synthesis

The two intervention points are:

`retrieve or skip → if retrieving, allocate relevance-guided execution`.

They should not be collapsed. Router quality requires calibration and harmful-retrieval accounting; execution quality requires matched candidate evidence, interface, model, and realized token/tool/latency budgets.

## Evidence audit

The month supports two early signals only. The router study exposes task-dependent operating points and retrieval harm. RARG++ provides strong tool-count contrasts and a negative speed/quality trade-off. Neither alone shows that adaptive retrieval is globally preferable to a matched fixed policy across domains and budgets.

## Questions carried forward

1. Can one router transfer its threshold while preserving both utility ranking and calibration?
2. Does relevance-guided execution still win when reranking, context, tools, and latency are fully charged?
3. Can retrieve/skip and post-retrieval allocation be optimized independently under one shared budget?
