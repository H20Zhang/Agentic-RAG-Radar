# Agentic RAG Weekly — 2026-W32

> **Coverage:** 2026-08-03 to 2026-08-09 · **Status:** finalized, revised 2026-08-15 · **Adjacent context:** older work is included only where it changes how this week's papers should be interpreted.

## Week thesis

**W32 is a convergence and stress-test of Agentic RAG's control stack—not the origin of its component ideas.**

The week makes five variables unusually visible: **retrieval environment/interface, explicit state, adaptive policy, resource allocation, and training-signal construction**. Historical backfill makes the chronology stricter: by May–July, DCI, RISE, DR-DCI, and RARG had already turned corpus-interface resolution, bounded/dynamic interaction spaces, and relevance-guided interaction into explicit research objects. S2G-RAG already had missing-information state; SPARKLE already provided adaptive/search-RL baselines; Active-RAG evaluation already made realized usage/controller cost first-class.

The useful question is therefore:

> **What does a W32 system add after matching the corpus interface, harness, explicit state, adaptive baseline, realized resource budget, and—when learning is involved—the training distribution itself?**

## Three durable shifts

### 1. The interface is becoming factorable, not merely richer

**READ** replaces dense top-k with lexical search, structural navigation, and bounded reads. **DocNavRAG** couples document-native navigation with collected/missing evidence state. **ACE-GraphRAG** makes graph context construction a policy. **SIEVE** adds a different decomposition: candidate eligibility, ranking, pre-read inspection, and reading granularity become separate control surfaces.

The stronger pre-August lineage remains essential. **DCI** showed that the bottleneck may be the resolution of the agent-facing corpus interface even after relevant documents are retrievable. **RISE** restored scale by making retrieval construct a persistent bounded interaction space. **DR-DCI** made that workspace dynamically expandable, and **RARG** reintroduced relevance as an execution prior inside interaction.

SIEVE matters because it does not simply make the interface “more agentic.” Its matched **Search–Fetch** control preserves the same ranker, result depth, and section-level reading while removing BQL candidate selection. That begins to isolate one interface variable instead of comparing two complete stacks. Its frequent zero-result fallback is equally important: exact eligibility constraints are useful only together with recovery from over-constrained search.

So the W32 delta is not “moving beyond top-k.” It is learning to **factor the information interface into independently testable decisions**.

### 2. Learning is expanding from policy optimization to supervision-distribution design

**S2G-RAG** predicts evidence sufficiency and structured missing-information gaps; its no-judge ablation keeps the retrieval budget/controller envelope much closer to fixed. This makes DocNavRAG's defensible delta **document-structured environment + evidence-state co-design**, not missing-evidence state itself.

**SAGE** learns per-query passage budget under a latency SLO, but its main baseline family is static-k. **SPARKLE** shows adaptive/search-RL comparisons are already feasible, so future learned-controller claims need an adaptive-vs-adaptive baseline rather than a generic fixed-versus-adaptive story.

**SearchMaster** changes a different layer: the **self-generated training distribution**. Evidence-Chain Generation reduces pseudo multi-hop tasks; Search-Depth Reward defines difficulty using the easiest successful search trajectory; the Over-Opening Penalty suppresses a drift toward redundant reads. The same-backbone naive-self-play baseline is the critical comparison: it shows that “more self-play” is not the whole explanation.

This matters because a self-improving search agent can optimize a bad curriculum perfectly. Task generation, verification, and reward semantics therefore belong inside the causal model, not outside it as data plumbing.

### 3. Evaluation needs resource, harness, and intervention matching

**Know Before You Fetch** separates retrieval calls, passage volume, and latency. **When Should Active RAG Retrieve?** shows nominal target usage is not the same as held-out realized usage and exposes controller/probe cost.

The backfilled **Is Grep All You Need?** adds a different confound: changing harness or tool-result delivery can materially shift—and sometimes reverse—grep-versus-vector conclusions. A retrieval result is therefore not cleanly attributable to the retriever if the surrounding evidence-delivery path changes.

SIEVE and SearchMaster strengthen the positive side of this lesson. SIEVE uses a matched interface control; SearchMaster uses same-backbone self-play/component ablations. These are more informative than another cross-system leaderboard because they remove more alternative explanations.

## Papers that matter most this week

**Forgotten History or Test-of-Time? — ★★★★☆.** Raises the novelty baseline to classical IR/QA; historical continuity does not mean technical equivalence.

**READ — ★★★★☆.** Shows the retrieval primitive/interface can dominate the agent loop; BM25 being statistically indistinguishable from READ is the most important negative result.

**SIEVE — ★★★★☆.** Factorizes candidate admissibility, ranking, inspection, and selective reading, with a matched Search–Fetch control that makes the Boolean-selection delta unusually identifiable.

**SearchMaster — ★★★★☆.** Makes self-play task/trajectory quality a first-class learning problem; same-backbone naive-self-play and component ablations provide stronger causal evidence than the heterogeneous external leaderboard.

**DocNavRAG — ★★★★☆.** Makes document structure and evidence progress jointly controllable; after stronger backfill, its novelty is environment–state co-design.

SAGE remains useful as a systems operating-point paper, but its static-k baseline family makes its learned-policy attribution weaker than the controls above.

## Evidence audit

The minimum credible causal lens is now:

`substrate × corpus boundary/interface resolution × harness/delivery × state × policy × realized resources × base model × training distribution × historical baseline`

Five confounds deserve explicit checks: a richer interface can masquerade as a better policy; harness/delivery can change retriever ranking; explicit state can be credited for gains caused by extra interaction budget; nominally identical budgets can realize different calls/tokens/latency/controller cost; and self-play can improve because the generated tasks/curriculum changed rather than because the optimizer learned a better control rule.

SIEVE's Search–Fetch control and SearchMaster's naive-self-play/component ablations are unusually useful because they remove some of these confounds. Their remaining caveats are concrete: SIEVE frequently falls back after over-constrained queries, while SearchMaster pays substantial proposer/solver rollout and verifier cost.

## Minimal reading order

For interface chronology: **A-RAG → DCI → RISE → DR-DCI → RARG → SIEVE → READ / DocNavRAG**.  
For state/learning: **S2G-RAG → SPARKLE → SearchMaster → SAGE**.  
For evaluation: **Is Grep All You Need? → Know Before You Fetch → When Should Active RAG Retrieve? → Forgotten History or Test-of-Time?**.

## Questions to carry forward

1. Can one benchmark independently vary **candidate admissibility, ranker, inspection surface, read granularity, state, policy, and resources** without changing the answer model?
2. For self-improving search agents, how do we separate a better **policy** from a better **training-task distribution/curriculum**?
3. Which modern gains remain after implementing strong historical control ideas with today's models and interaction environments?
