## 1. Explicit evidence-path operations gain cross-task support

VisDocAgentBench keeps a top-10 opaque page-output contract while testing static versus iterative visual target discovery. CTIFoundry keeps a stock mini-swe-agent but replaces flat-file access with materialized entities/relations and typed `resolve / traverse / collect / read` operations.

The two treatments are independent enough to reinforce one map-level claim: **whether an evidence path is expressible and inspectable is part of the system, not neutral plumbing**.

The ceiling remains medium. VisDoc's full visual agent accumulates about 177K input tokens versus 101K for its no-iteration control, and important static/agentic comparisons use different retrieval backends. CTIFoundry bundles corpus materialization, seven tools, output descriptions, system prompt, and task skills.

## 2. Evidence sufficiency may route breadth versus depth

D2-ScaleAgent's verifier reads an Evidence Bank and chooses whether an unresolved question requires another page or finer inspection of a found page. On GPT-4o/MMLongBench, the full package scores 52.0 versus 44.1 without the verifier and 46.8 without retrieval scaling.

This remains a single-paper early signal. The ablations change policy, evidence state, operations, and realized work together; key external controls do not match the package's 21.4K tokens, 5.02 routing-agent calls, and 16.22 seconds.

## 3. Capability coverage may fail before planning

ToolScout's specialist retriever reaches 91.8% matched-source depth-20 coverage but only 22.3% on mixed sources; routed aggregation reaches 86.1%. The result places a candidate-capability audit upstream of agent planning.

The study stops at candidate coverage and a generation proxy. It does not execute tools to establish end-task gains, and “source style” still mixes query–tool pairing with target-side distribution.
