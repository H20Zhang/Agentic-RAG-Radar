# Agentic RAG Weekly — 2026-W34

> **Coverage:** 2026-08-17 to 2026-08-23 · **Status:** rolling · **Current evidence through:** 2026-08-18

## Week thesis

**W34 makes “where retrieval intelligence lives” more concrete: the system must choose when to materialize evidence, what progress/state to keep observable, and whether apparent context savings are simply repaid through re-querying.**

The falsifiable version is:

> **Moving work out of an index or prompt is only a systems improvement if the displaced work does not reappear as larger query-time localization, controller, or reacquisition cost at the same answer/evidence quality.**

LENS moves evidence boundaries/materialization to query time. RAAC adds trajectory-progress signals to decide continue/redirect/stop. The context-compression study shows the opposite failure mode: state removed from the prompt can reappear as extra retrieval without changing completion.

## Three shifts

### 1. Evidence materialization becomes a placement decision

LENS treats candidate evidence regions over dynamic raw documents as latent until the query arrives. That buys freshness and query-dependent granularity without a persistent index.

The negative result prevents an “index-free wins” story. ReAct has higher D500 EM (65.2 vs 62.4), and fullwiki answer EM is nearly tied. LENS wins more clearly on evidence recall/grounding and stale-index resilience while spending more online work.

**So what:** the right comparison is lifecycle cost + evidence fidelity, not preprocessing count.

### 2. Long search needs progress observability, not merely more history

RAAC measures criteria coverage, document novelty, query diversity, and drift, then overlays `continue / intervene / stop` on seven existing DRAs.

Its same-agent comparisons are stronger than a new end-to-end architecture: on BrowseComp-Plus the paper reports 14 fewer searches on average and ~3 points average accuracy improvement. But controller/re-thinker LLM calls are outside the search-call metric, and some cells get worse.

**So what:** “adaptive stopping saves calls” is incomplete until controller compute is included.

### 3. State compression can create a hidden retrieval tax

The compression study keeps the interaction horizon fixed and decomposes tool use into retrieval/reacquisition versus execution. At 5× compression, retrieval rises in all six model-regime cells while completion is statistically unchanged; oracle restoration of dropped queryable state removes most of the added interaction.

ALFWorld is the crucial negative: the same compression does not cause a retrieval surge when relevant state can be re-observed cheaply.

**So what:** the important property of agent state is not only size, but **recoverability × reacquisition cost**.

## Core tension

Three resource placements can substitute for one another:

`offline materialization ↔ query-time localization ↔ retained context/state ↔ external reacquisition`

Optimizing any one number can therefore create a false efficiency claim. “No index,” “fewer searches,” or “smaller context” can each move work to another stage.

The missing systems metric is **lifecycle work at matched evidence/answer quality**.

## Evidence audit

**Strongest causal evidence:** RAAC's within-agent overlay comparisons; LENS's stale-index and sequential-exploration ablations; context compression's oracle state-restoration intervention.

**Weak spots:** LENS does not improve answer EM against ReAct in the main controlled comparisons; RAAC search-call accounting omits controller/re-thinker model cost; context-compression evidence is strongest in one bounded environment and explicitly fails to reproduce in ALFWorld.

These negatives are not footnotes—they locate the boundary of each claim.
