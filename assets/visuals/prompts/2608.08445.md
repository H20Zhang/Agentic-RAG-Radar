## Visual question

What in modern Agentic RAG is genuinely new, and what is a new implementation of older closed-loop IR/QA ideas?

## Grounded mechanism

Allowed elements:

- classical retrieval-based QA as a muted single-pass reference;
- QUALIFIER-style loop: structured constraints → retrieve/extract → verify → relax/reformulate → repeat or NIL;
- modern Agentic RAG loop with retrieval/tool action, evidence observation, state update, and stopping;
- a bridge labeled `LLM interface / modeling regime`, showing continuity without claiming identity.

## Research delta to emphasize

**The novelty baseline is older than modern RAG.** Query reformulation, verification, and iterative retrieval have classical IR/QA antecedents; modern claims should isolate what LLM-era interfaces, scale, learning, or state add.

## Compared with

LLM-centric histories that treat adaptive retrieval and iterative verification as ideas born with modern RAG.

## Do not imply

- Do not imply QUALIFIER and modern LLM agents are technically equivalent.
- Do not imply historical iterative refinement was causally proven to explain QUALIFIER's TREC ranking.
- Do not turn the figure into a timeline of every RAG paper.
- Do not put benchmark counts or dates in the generated image.

## Art direction

Wide conceptual continuity diagram. Left/center: a compact classical QA loop with `retrieve`, `verify`, `relax/reformulate`. Right: a modern agent loop with `tool action`, `evidence`, `state`, `stop`. A single bridge should say `new interface / regime`. Minimal academic infographic; the central message is continuity plus changed implementation, not “nothing is new.”
