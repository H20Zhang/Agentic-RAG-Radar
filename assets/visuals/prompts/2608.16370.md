## Visual question

How can context compression leave task completion unchanged while forcing an agent to spend much more interaction on retrieval?

## Grounded mechanism

Allowed elements:

- full/fact-preserving context keeps execution-relevant state available and lets the agent spend most interaction on task work;
- sliding compression drops some execution-relevant state;
- missing **queryable** state triggers retrieval/re-query to reacquire it;
- retrieval and execution both consume the same bounded interaction horizon;
- an oracle restoration branch injects dropped queryable state and removes most of the added retrieval;
- ALFWorld is a boundary condition where the same compression does not produce a retrieval surge.

## Research delta to emphasize

**Compression can shift cost from context tokens into external state reacquisition without changing the final completion metric.**

## Compared with

Full context and fact-preserving compression under the same bounded-horizon tool-use protocol.

## Do not imply

- Do not imply all context compression increases retrieval; ALFWorld is a negative boundary.
- Do not imply completion is useless; the claim is that completion alone hides interaction cost.
- Do not imply retrieval calls equal wall-clock or monetary cost.
- Do not imply the paper proposes a new compression policy.
- Do not put p-values, model names, or benchmark numbers in the image.

## Art direction

Wide failure/evidence map. Use at most 7 short labels such as `full state`, `compressed`, `missing state`, `re-query`, `execute`, `restore`, `complete`. Show a clear reacquisition loop and a bypass when state is restored. No dashboard or benchmark chart.
