## Visual question

How does SIEVE separate candidate eligibility, ranking, result inspection, and reading granularity instead of collapsing them into one Search–Visit path?

## Grounded mechanism

Allowed elements:

- one structured document collection with addressable title/metadata/section fields;
- an agent formulates a fielded Boolean constraint;
- Boolean selection defines an eligible candidate set;
- a separate interchangeable ranker orders only that set;
- compact result cards expose titles/headings/query-focused snippets;
- the agent chooses one named section to fetch;
- fetched evidence informs the next query or final answer;
- zero-result search may fall back to unconstrained lexical ranking, but this should be a small caveat rather than the dominant diagram.

## Research delta to emphasize

**Selection, ranking, inspection, and reading become separate control surfaces.** Structure survives the whole interaction instead of being discarded after retrieval.

## Compared with

Conventional `search → visit whole page`, and the matched Search–Fetch control where section-level reading remains but BQL candidate selection is removed.

## Do not imply

- Do not imply Boolean filtering replaces ranking; ranking remains separate and modular.
- Do not imply every BQL query succeeds without fallback.
- Do not imply gains come only from Boolean syntax; result inspection and section-level fetch are separate components.
- Do not copy the paper's original figure composition.
- Do not include benchmark numbers, token percentages, dates, citations, or paper IDs in the image.

## Art direction

Wide before/after research interface diagram. Left muted: `search → result list → whole page`. Right dominant: `constraints → eligible set → rank → inspect → section fetch → evidence`, with feedback from evidence to the next constraints. Use simple document/section geometry, not browser chrome. Minimal labels: `constraints`, `eligible`, `rank`, `inspect`, `fetch`, `evidence`.
