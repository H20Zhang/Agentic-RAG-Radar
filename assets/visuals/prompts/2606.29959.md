## Visual question

How does calibrated confidence become an interface for choosing no retrieval, compact retrieval, full retrieval, or abstention?

## Grounded mechanism

Allowed elements:

- closed-book probe;
- calibrated probability of correctness;
- three retrieval actions: `k=0`, `k=1`, `k=5`;
- optional abstain after chosen-answer confidence;
- separate cost lenses for `retrieval call`, `passage/context budget`, and `latency`.

## Research delta to emphasize

**Adaptive retrieval has multiple cost axes.** Compact retrieval can save passages without saving a retrieval call, and the confidence probe itself can dominate latency in some model regimes.

## Compared with

Always-retrieve RAG, binary retrieve/not-retrieve gating, and raw uncertainty signals such as TARG prefix entropy/margin.

## Do not imply

- Do not imply `k=1` is free or avoids retrieval.
- Do not imply gating is always faster; the paper reports both slower and faster latency regimes.
- Do not present Self-RAG or Adaptive-RAG as fully reproduced end-to-end baselines.
- Do not put benchmark numbers in the generated image.

## Art direction

Wide decision-interface diagram: `probe → calibrate → {no retrieval | compact | full} → answer`, with a small abstain exit. Under the actions, show three independent ruler-like resource lenses labeled `calls`, `passages`, `latency`. Minimal academic infographic; make the accounting distinction visually dominant.
