## Visual question

How does SAGE turn retrieval depth into a learned per-query resource-allocation decision under a latency SLO?

## Grounded mechanism

Allowed elements:

- one fixed hybrid retrieval substrate;
- a small probe retrieval producing score/rank and sparse–dense agreement features;
- a learned lightweight policy;
- policy chooses a small / medium / large passage budget `k`;
- selected top-k context goes to the LLM once;
- latency/SLO outcomes used for offline supervision/calibration, not an online reasoning loop.

## Research delta to emphasize

**Retrieval budget becomes a control action rather than a global constant.** The policy changes how much evidence is purchased per query while leaving the underlying retrieval stack largely fixed.

## Compared with

Static-k hybrid RAG. Adaptive-k and Stop-RAG are relevant prior art and should appear only as a caveat, not as methods the paper experimentally beats.

## Do not imply

- Do not depict repeated LLM search/reasoning rounds; SAGE selects k before generation.
- Do not imply the policy eliminates the probe-retrieval cost.
- Do not claim superiority over Adaptive-k or Stop-RAG.
- Do not put benchmark numbers or dollar savings into the image.

## Art direction

Wide resource-allocation diagram. Left: muted `same k for every query`. Right: `probe → features → policy → choose k → context → answer`, with three clearly different budget widths. A subtle `SLO / latency` arrow should point to offline policy learning/calibration, not the online answer loop. Minimal labels: `probe`, `features`, `policy`, `budget k`, `SLO`.
