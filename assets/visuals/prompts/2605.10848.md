# Visual brief — Pi-Serini

## Visual question

How does Pi-Serini separate lexical ranking quality from the agent-facing interface and retrieval depth when asking whether BM25 is sufficient for deep research?

## Grounded mechanism

Show one query entering a tuned BM25 backend that produces a deep cached ranking. The agent can then use three operations over that ranking: search to create/cache a ranking, browse/read-search-results to inspect deeper ranks without issuing another backend query, and read-document to inspect selected documents in bounded chunks. The core distinction is between **evidence surfaced by the backend** and **evidence actually previewed/read by the agent**.

## Research delta

The visual should make clear that the paper is not simply “BM25 beats dense retrieval.” Its experimental intervention combines stronger BM25 configuration, much deeper surfaced rankings, and an interface that lets the agent browse/read those rankings without dumping all candidates into context.

## Compared with

Released BrowseComp-Plus agent baselines using default/shallow BM25 or dense retrievers, plus the paper's own BM25-tuning and retrieval-depth ablations.

## Do not imply

- Do not imply lexical retrieval is universally superior to dense retrieval.
- Do not imply deeper ranking automatically means the agent inspects more relevant evidence; previewed recall saturates much earlier than surfaced recall in the reported ablation.
- Do not imply the 18-point BM25-tuning gain is established on the full 830-query benchmark; the ablations are on a 100-query subset.
- Do not hide the strong-model and single-benchmark dependence.
