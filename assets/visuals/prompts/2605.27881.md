# Visual brief — Retrieval, Reward, and Training Protocols

**Renderer:** GPT-image-gen  
**Status:** pending  
**Grounding:** full-text checked

## Visual question

Which apparent search-agent learning gains disappear or reverse when the retrieval environment and training protocol are controlled?

## Grounded mechanism

Allowed elements:

- same search-agent loop in the center;
- four surrounding causal controls: `corpus coverage`, `reward`, `tool format`, `search budget`;
- show an incomplete corpus causing missing evidence but a positive final-answer reward through parametric knowledge;
- show process reward changing query quality / redundancy / search depth rather than uniformly improving all of them;
- show longer budget not guaranteeing higher accuracy.

## Research delta to emphasize

**Training environment and protocol can dominate the nominal RL algorithm.** A better-looking objective is not causal if evidence coverage, tool syntax, rollout freshness, or search budget differ.

## Compared with

Search-R1 outcome reward versus GiGPO, IGPO, and Tree-GRPO process-credit methods under controlled retrieval/training settings.

## Do not imply

- Do not imply outcome reward is universally optimal.
- Do not imply all process rewards are harmful; some win particular settings or behavior metrics.
- Do not invent benchmark values inside the image.
- Do not turn the figure into a generic RL taxonomy; focus on causal confounding around the same search loop.

## Art direction

Use a compact failure/evidence map, not a pipeline poster. Keep 5–7 labels: `corpus coverage`, `search loop`, `reward`, `tool format`, `budget`, `spurious reward`, `behavior drift`. Clean academic style, no dashboards or decorative robots.
